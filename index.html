Space Archaeology

Relics of the Cosmos

From personal mementos to critical scientific tools, explore the fascinating items NASA astronauts and rovers left behind on the Moon and Mars.

The Moon Apollo 14 • 1971

Surveyor Landers

Mission Milestones: To demonstrate a soft landing on the Moon and conduct surface experiments.

Mission Duration: Successfully landed, transmitted photos, conducted experiments, and proved lunar soil could support an Apollo Lunar Module.

Final Communication: Parts of the spacecraft were retrieved by Apollo 12 astronauts to study the effects of lunar exposure.

Sites: Sinus Medii, Oceanus Procellarum, Mare Tranquillitatis
The Moon Apollo 16 • 1972

Apollo Lunar Modules

Mission Milestones: The Apollo 11 Lunar Module “Eagle” carried astronauts to the first crewed lunar landing.

Mission Duration: The EASEP, deployed on the Moon’s surface, included a seismometer and dust detector to transmit lunar data back to Earth.

Final Communication: After the moonwalk and sample collection, the astronauts used the ascent stage to return to the Command Module, leaving the descent stage and EASEP on the Moon.

Sites: Descartes Highlands, Mare Tranquillitatis, Taurus-Littrow
The Moon Apollo 15 • 1971

Lunar Roving Vehicles

Purpose of LRV: To expand the exploration range of astronauts on the Moon.

Technical Specifications:Lightweight, foldable, electric vehicle with a payload capacity of over 210 kg, powered by two 36-volt batteries.

Operational History: Used on Apollo 15, 16, and 17 missions, enabling astronauts to cover over 90 kilometers combined.

Sites: Hadley-Apennine, Descartes Highlands, Taurus-Littrow
Mars February 18 • 2021

Ingenuity Mars Helicopter

Mission Milestones: To demonstrate powered, controlled flight in Mars’ thin atmosphere.

Mission Duration: April 19, 2021, becoming the first aircraft to fly on another planet.

Final Communication: January 18, 2024, after a communication issue and rotor blade damage.

Site: Jezero Crater on Mars
Mars March 2 • 1972

Pioneer 10 & 11

Mission Milestones: First spacecraft to navigate the asteroid belt, obtain close-up images of Jupiter, and cross Neptune’s orbit.

Mission Duration: Operated for over 30 years, far exceeding its original 21-month design.

Final Communication: Sent its final signal back to Earth in January 2003 from 7.6 billion miles away.

Site: Interstellar deep space
Mars March 6 • 2009

Kelper Space Telescope

Kepler Mission Impact: Revolutionized our understanding of the cosmos by discovering over 2,600 exoplanets.

Exoplanet Discovery: Proved that there are more planets than stars in the night sky.

Potentially Habitable Planets: Revealed that up to half of visible stars may host potentially habitable, Earth-sized planets.

Site: Heliocentric orbit path, continuously viewing a specific star field in the constellations Cygnus and Lyra.
